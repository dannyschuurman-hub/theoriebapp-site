Leaving a driveway: do pedestrians go first?

Theorie B editorial team · 16 November 2026 · 2 min read

In this article

Special roads and manoeuvres ↗Test yourself ↓

Short answer

When leaving a driveway, let all other traffic go first. That includes a runner on the pavement.

You're driving out of a clearly recognisable driveway. To reach the road, you need to cross the pavement, where a runner is approaching. Should the runner wait for you to pull out?

Check for all traffic when leaving a driveway

The rule for leaving a driveway goes beyond waiting for other cars. You must let all other traffic go first. That includes pedestrians.

Look beyond the carriageway you want to join. Check the pavement you are crossing too. Someone walking or running may cross your path before your car reaches the carriageway.

This situation specifies a clearly recognisable driveway. You do not need to decide whether the location might be an ordinary junction instead.

An example when pulling out

You're driving out of a driveway and approaching the pavement. A runner is coming from the left. There are no cars on the carriageway at that moment.

You still do not drive straight out. Wait before crossing the runner's path and let them pass. Then check whether you can continue leaving the driveway.

An empty carriageway does not mean all other traffic has passed. The pavement is part of what you need to check before moving on.

The misconception: I'm coming from the right

You may be approaching from the runner's right. That does not change this situation. You are leaving a driveway and therefore carrying out a special manoeuvre.

Creeping forwards or sounding your horn does not give you the right to go first either. The runner does not have to stop to let you pull out.

Remember the full instruction when leaving a driveway: let all other traffic go first. Looking only for cyclists and cars misses an important part of the rule.

The ruleRVV 1990, art. 54
