Can you hold your phone for navigation while driving?

Theorie B editorial team · 16 November 2026 · 2 min read

In this article

Responsible driving ↗Test yourself ↓

Short answer

You must not hold a mobile electronic device while driving, even to check directions. The rule applies whether you are making a call, reading a message or checking a map.

You're driving somewhere new. Your navigation says a turn is coming up, but you're unsure which street to take. Can you pick up your phone for a quick look at the map?

The rule is about holding the device

People often describe this as a ban on making calls at the wheel. That can make other uses of a phone sound acceptable. The ban actually covers holding a mobile electronic device while driving.

You do not need to be on a call to break the rule. Picking up your phone to check how many minutes are left on your journey counts too. A brief glance makes no difference.

An example on the road

You're driving through a residential area. Your phone is on the passenger seat and gives a spoken instruction. You didn't quite catch it, so you pick it up, hold it above the steering wheel and check the next turn.

You are still holding a phone while driving. Using it for navigation makes no difference to this rule.

Set your route before you leave. If you miss a turn, keep watching the traffic. Find a suitable place to park if you need to check the route again.

The misconception: I'm not making a call

The function you are using does not determine whether you may hold the device. Keeping your other hand on the wheel does not help either. The rule has no exception for driving with one hand on the wheel.

Focus on the word holding. That saves you trying to work out separately whether each app falls within the ban.

The ruleRVV 1990, art. 61a

Try it / Practice question

You are driving a car and holding your phone to check a route. Is this allowed while driving?

You must not hold a mobile electronic device while driving. This includes holding a phone to check a route.
