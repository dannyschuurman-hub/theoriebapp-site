Does waiting in your car count as parking?

Theorie B editorial team · 17 November 2026 · 3 min read

In this article

Legislation ↗Test yourself ↓

Short answer

Waiting for someone who is still indoors and is not getting in immediately counts as parking. Staying in the car does not change that.

You pull up at the roadside to collect someone who is still indoors. You stay behind the wheel and wait ten minutes, watching for the door to open. You are not loading anything and nobody is getting in immediately. That waiting counts as parking.

Waiting is different from someone getting straight in

Picking someone up involves two distinct stages. First you wait for them to be ready. Then they get in. Staying in the same place for both does not make the waiting part of the immediate boarding process.

Look at what is happening beside your car. Is the person you are collecting still indoors? Then you are waiting for them to come out. The purpose of your journey does not, by itself, determine what your car is doing at that moment.

An example when collecting someone

You've arranged to collect a friend. When you arrive, he isn't ready. You pull up at the roadside. Ten minutes later, you still haven't seen him. Your hands are on the wheel and you could drive away straight away.

That may feel different from leaving your car and going inside for a visit. But where you sit does not determine whether you are parking. A car can be parked with its driver still in the seat.

The same applies if you are ready to leave as soon as your friend appears. You are still waiting for him to arrive. What is happening beside the car changes only when he actually gets in.

The misconception: if I stay in the car, I'm not parking

You do not have to lock the car for it to count as parking. Locking the doors is not one of the conditions used to assess this waiting. Leaving them unlocked changes nothing about the situation.

A running engine does not turn waiting into someone getting straight in either. The engine noise may suggest you are about to leave, but it does not bring the person you are collecting to the car.

Even if you call it just stopping for a moment, look again at what you are doing. Here, the car is at the roadside for ten minutes without loading or anyone immediately getting in or out. Those are the details that matter when identifying parking.

The ruleRVV 1990, art. 1, begrip parkeren

Try it / Practice question

You leave your car at the roadside and wait ten minutes for someone still indoors. You are not loading anything and nobody is boarding immediately. Does this legally count as parking?

Waiting for someone is not immediate boarding or alighting. Staying in the car does not stop it counting as parking.
