Missed your exit: can you reverse on the hard shoulder?

Theorie B editorial team · 22 November 2026 · 3 min read

In this article

Special roads and manoeuvres ↗Test yourself ↓

Short answer

You must not reverse on a motorway. This includes the hard shoulder. Missing your exit does not create an exception to the rule.

You're on the motorway and spot your exit too late. The turn-off is just behind you. It looks like only a short distance back along the hard shoulder, and you cannot see anyone approaching. You still must not reverse. The motorway ban on reversing includes the hard shoulder.

The rule includes the hard shoulder

A hard shoulder can look like an empty strip beside the traffic. That may make it seem like space to correct your mistake. But for the ban on reversing, you cannot treat it as separate from the motorway.

Moving from a normal lane to the hard shoulder would change your position within the road. The action you are planning would remain the same: reversing to get back to the exit.

Your destination makes no difference either. You want to take the exit you have just passed. That explains why you want to go back, but it does not give you an exception. Missing an exit does not make the planned movement permissible.

An example just after an exit

You have just missed the exit. At that moment, you cannot see any other traffic in your mirror. You look at the hard shoulder and think the short distance would be easy to cover.

That empty space can mislead you. You can see whether someone is approaching now, but that does not determine whether you may reverse. The ban here does not depend on other cars being present.

Perhaps you plan to switch on your hazard warning lights first. Those do not give you permission either. They change neither the direction you would be travelling in nor the fact that you are on a motorway.

The misconception: a short distance back is fine

If you can still see the exit, reversing may feel like a small correction. You have only just passed it. But there is no short distance within which this manoeuvre becomes allowed.

There is no point estimating the metres back to the exit. Nor does the thought that you would finish quickly create an exception. The length and duration of the movement do not change what you would be doing.

When you miss an exit, separate what you want from what the traffic rule allows. You want to return to a point behind you. Consider what that would involve and the type of road you are on. This helps you avoid letting a nearby exit decide your judgement.

The ruleRVV 1990, art. 43 lid 1

Try it / Practice question

You miss an exit on the motorway. May you reverse along the hard shoulder to reach the exit?

Reversing is prohibited on a motorway. This also applies to the hard shoulder.
