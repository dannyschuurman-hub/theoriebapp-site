Can you sound your horn to say hello?

Theorie B editorial team · 23 November 2026 · 3 min read

In this article

Responsible driving ↗Test yourself ↓

Short answer

You may give an audible warning only to avert imminent danger. You must not sound your horn to greet someone when there is no danger.

You're driving along a street and spot someone you know on the pavement. They haven't seen you. You want to give a quick beep to let them know you're passing. There is no danger. You must not use the horn as a greeting.

The purpose of the sound determines the rule

You may give an audible warning only to avert imminent danger. Consider why you are reaching for the horn. What do you want the sound to achieve?

When greeting someone, you want them to look up and recognise you. You are attracting their attention to make contact. That is a different purpose from averting danger. However friendly your intention, it does not meet the condition for giving an audible warning.

Knowing the person makes no difference. Seeing someone familiar on the pavement does not, in itself, present imminent danger. Recognising them prompts your greeting, but does not give you permission to sound the horn.

An example on the road

You see a friend standing by a front door. He is looking the other way. You think a quick beep would let him know it is you. You would only need to press the horn briefly.

Think through that moment. Your friend hasn't seen you and you want his attention. Nothing else is happening. The sound would simply make him look up as you drive past.

A quick tap may feel harmless. But you do not assess an audible warning solely by how long it lasts. Start with its purpose. Releasing the horn quickly does not turn a greeting into a warning of danger.

The misconception: one short beep is always allowed

The word short can distract you from the reason for sounding the horn. You start thinking mainly about how much noise you make. A shorter sound does not remove the condition about imminent danger.

Being outside a built-up area does not let you use the horn freely to say hello either. The location creates no exception for that greeting. Move the example from a street to somewhere outside a built-up area, and your intention remains the same.

Focus on what is happening before you want to sound the horn. Have you simply seen someone you know and want to make contact? Then it is a greeting. The length of the sound and where you are driving do not turn it into an action to avert imminent danger.

The ruleRVV 1990, art. 28

Try it / Practice question

You see someone you know on the pavement. There is no danger. May you sound your horn to say hello?

You may sound your horn only to avert imminent danger.
