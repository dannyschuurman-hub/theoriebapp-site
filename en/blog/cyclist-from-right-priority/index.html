Cyclist coming from the right: who has priority?

Theorie B editorial team · 19 November 2026 · 3 min read

In this article

Priority and giving way ↗Test yourself ↓

Short answer

At a junction with paved roads and no signs, traffic lights or give-way triangles, give way to drivers and riders from the right. That includes cyclists.

You're approaching a junction in a residential area by car. Both roads are paved. There are no signs, traffic lights or give-way triangles, the triangular road markings known in Dutch as haaientanden. A cyclist approaches from the street on your right. You give way: at this junction, drivers and riders from the right go first.

Cyclists count as drivers too

The Dutch legal term bestuurder may make you picture someone at the wheel of a car. It also includes someone riding a bicycle. The term covers more road users than motorists alone.

An approaching cyclist is therefore among the drivers and riders you consider when deciding priority. There is no need to invent a separate order for bicycles. What matters here is the direction the cyclist comes from.

Always check the junction itself, though. In this example, the roads are paved and there are no signs, traffic lights or give-way triangles. Those conditions belong together. The word cyclist alone does not tell you who goes first without looking at the junction.

An example in a residential area

You're driving along a quiet street. At the next junction, someone is cycling towards you from the right. You check the road and see no signs or markings controlling priority. The side road is paved too.

Now imagine a car coming from that same side road. You would consider where its driver was approaching from. Do the same for the cyclist. This helps you avoid unconsciously leaving bicycles out of the priority rule.

It can be tempting to focus mainly on other cars. For this rule, it makes no difference whether the person on your right is pedalling or sitting behind a steering wheel. Look carefully for the cyclist in the side road too.

The misconception: cars go before bicycles

A car is bigger than a bicycle. That does not give its driver a different place in the order here. Vehicle size does not determine who may go first at this junction.

The same applies to speed. Driving faster than the cyclist does not give you priority. Do not base your judgement on who could reach the junction first.

Do not extend this rule to every encounter with a cyclist either. Cyclists do not always have priority. Combine what you see: the type of junction and the direction the other person comes from. That lets you work out the order without relying on vehicle size or speed.

The ruleRVV 1990, art. 15 lid 1
