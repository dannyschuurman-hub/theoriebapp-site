Can you use your rear fog light in heavy rain?

Theorie B editorial team · 20 November 2026 · 3 min read

In this article

Safe driving and emergencies ↗Test yourself ↓

Short answer

Rain alone does not allow you to use your rear fog light, even with visibility of 40 metres. You may use it in fog or snow when visibility is less than 50 metres.

It is raining heavily and you can see only 40 metres ahead. You remember learning a 50-metre threshold for the rear fog light. But distance alone does not tell you whether you may switch it on. The cause of the poor visibility matters too.

The cause of poor visibility matters

You may use a rear fog light in fog or snow when visibility is less than 50 metres. Rain alone does not allow it, even if you can see only 40 metres ahead.

Consider two things together. First, what is reducing your visibility? Then, how far can you still see? The weather and the distance together form the conditions for using this light.

Be clear about which light you mean. This article concerns the rear fog light. Remembering only the general term fog lights can blur that distinction. Link these conditions to the light at the back of your car.

An example in a heavy downpour

You're driving through heavy rain and looking ahead. You cannot see further than about 40 metres. There is no fog or snow. Your windscreen wipers are on and you are considering switching on the rear fog light too.

Start with the weather you are driving in. The water reducing your visibility is rain. The distance is less than 50 metres, but that covers only one part of the rule. Reduced visibility does not change the type of precipitation.

Compare this with fog or snow in which you can see less than 50 metres. Both parts then match the conditions for using the rear fog light. Comparing the situations shows why remembering a number alone is not enough.

The misconception: it is always allowed below 50 metres

In poor visibility, the distance can take all your attention. Forty metres is less than fifty, so reaching for the switch may seem logical. Take a moment to consider the cause before drawing that conclusion.

Your wiper setting does not settle the question. It does not remove the condition about fog or snow. The wipers can be on in a rain shower while the rain still does not permit use of the rear fog light.

Learn the distance together with the weather conditions. This keeps the threshold linked to the situation it applies to, rather than turning it into a number you use for every kind of poor visibility.

The ruleRVV 1990, art. 34 lid 2

Try it / Practice question

It is raining heavily and visibility is down to 40 metres. May you switch on your rear fog light because of the rain alone?

A rear fog light may be used in fog or snow when visibility is below 50 metres. Rain alone does not allow its use.
