Amber traffic lights: should you stop or keep going?

Theorie B editorial team · 16 November 2026 · 2 min read

In this article

Traffic signs ↗Test yourself ↓

Short answer

Amber means stop. You may continue only if you can no longer reasonably stop safely. If you can stop safely before the stop line, you must do so.

You're approaching traffic lights. Just before you reach the junction, the light changes from green to amber. The junction is empty and you're in a hurry. Can you keep going?

Stopping is the starting point

An amber traffic light is not an invitation to rush across the junction. The starting point is that you stop. The exception depends on whether you can still do so safely.

Simply judging whether you can get across before the light turns red is not enough. The relevant question is whether you can still reasonably stop safely when amber appears.

An empty junction does not change the meaning of amber either. Seeing nobody approach is no reason to continue if you can stop safely.

An example at a junction

You're approaching a junction when the light turns amber. You have enough space to brake steadily and stop before the stop line.

You stop. There is no need to check for a camera or work out how much time accelerating might save. You can stop safely, so you follow the main rule.

Are you so close when amber appears that you can no longer reasonably stop safely? Then the exception applies and you may continue. There is no fixed distance for making this judgement here. The situation described determines whether stopping safely is still possible.

The misconception: amber gives me a little more time

Treating amber as extra time to keep going reverses the rule. Continuing is the exception when you can no longer reasonably stop safely.

Accelerating does not help you make that judgement. Focus on the moment the light turns amber: can you still stop safely before the stop line? The practice question below tells you that you can.
