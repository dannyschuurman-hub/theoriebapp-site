Red light on a priority road: must you stop?

Theorie B editorial team · 21 November 2026 · 3 min read

In this article

Traffic signs ↗Test yourself ↓

Short answer

You must stop at red even on a priority road. Traffic lights take precedence over signs controlling priority. A B1 sign does not allow you to drive through red.

You're approaching a junction on a priority road. You have passed sign B1, which indicates a priority road. Ahead, the traffic light for your direction is red. You stop at the light. Traffic lights take precedence over signs controlling priority.

The traffic light takes precedence over the priority sign

As you approach the junction, you see different traffic controls. The sign tells you that you are on a priority road. The traffic light for your direction shows red. To decide what to do, you need to understand how the two relate.

You cannot use the sign as permission to continue. The red light is also there and requires you to act. Your car must obey the same stop signal when you are on a priority road.

Keep the meanings of the sign and the light separate. A priority road tells you about priority. That does not give you permission to pass a red traffic light.

An example at a junction

You're approaching a junction with a side road on the right. Nobody is coming from it. There is no traffic on the junction itself either. It looks as though you could continue without getting in anyone's way.

Check the traffic light for your direction. It is still red. An empty road gives you no different instruction. You do not need to weigh up whether there is enough space to drive through anyway.

A car approaching from the right would make the junction look different. But the meaning of your red light would stay the same. Traffic in the side road does not determine what the light requires you to do here.

The misconception: I can keep going on a priority road

If you have just passed sign B1, it may stick in your mind. You know you are on a priority road and focus on the side roads. That can lead you to give too much weight to the sign you passed earlier.

Keep checking the light for your direction at the junction too. It is a separate traffic control that you must consider. Looking only at the priority sign gives you an incomplete picture of what is ahead.

Get into the habit of recognising both controls and knowing which takes precedence. You can then decide what to do as you approach, without letting an empty side road guide your judgement.

The ruleRVV 1990, art. 64 en art. 68 lid 1 onder c, bijlage 1, bord B1
